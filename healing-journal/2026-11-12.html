A moment for yourself

November 12, 2026 · Curiosity & the courage to ask · 3 min read

Let the obvious question have a voice

There is a particular little sentence that can make a simple question feel surprisingly heavy: I should already know this. It appears while reading instructions, joining a conversation, trying something new, or staring at a button that everyone else seems to understand. Suddenly curiosity is standing outside the door, politely holding its coat, while pride pretends everything is perfectly clear. ✨

Adults become skilled at looking informed. We nod at the right moment. We collect context from the next three sentences and hope nobody asks us to explain the first one. Sometimes this works beautifully. Sometimes we leave with no idea what happened and a very convincing expression on our face. There is humor in that, but also tenderness. Most of us want to belong before we feel ready to reveal what we do not know.

A question does not erase your intelligence. It is one of the ways intelligence moves. Asking can mean you are paying attention closely enough to notice the missing piece. It can mean you care about understanding rather than merely appearing to understand. Even the Moon spends part of every cycle out of sight; not everything meaningful has to arrive fully illuminated. 🌙

Perhaps today you can practice with something small. Ask what a word means. Request the directions one more time. Say, ‘I understand this part, but I am missing what comes next.’ A clear question gives another person something useful to answer. It also saves you from constructing an entire bridge out of guesses and then acting surprised when it leads to the linen cupboard. 🌿

You are allowed to be new at something.

Of course, not every room welcomes questions kindly. Some people answer with impatience, or use knowledge as a way to feel taller. Their response may tell you something about the room; it does not decide the worth of your curiosity. You can seek another source, ask someone safer, or return later. Courage does not require offering your uncertainty to people who handle it carelessly. ⭐

Questions can also be directed inward, with gentleness. What am I assuming here? What would help me feel more prepared? Do I actually want this, or have I been following an old answer because it was already written down? Curiosity creates space between habit and choice. It does not demand an immediate revelation. Sometimes the honest answer is simply, I need more time to notice.

There may be questions with no tidy response today. A relationship can remain complicated. A decision can hold two real possibilities. A feeling can resist being named. You do not have to force uncertainty into a neat little box and tie it with ribbon. Let the question breathe. Write it down. Walk beside it for a while. A crystal ball is not required; careful attention is much more available. 🔮

Today, notice one place where pretending to know is taking more energy than asking. Give the obvious question a voice, even if it comes out quietly. You may receive information, reassurance, a better choice, or the comforting discovery that several other people were wondering the same thing. Curiosity is not a gap in who you are. It is a doorway through which your life can keep becoming wider, clearer, and more honestly your own. 🤍

Carry this into today

What honest question could make a little more room for understanding today?

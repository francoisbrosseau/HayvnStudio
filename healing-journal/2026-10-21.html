A moment for yourself

October 21, 2026 · Meaningful choices · 3 min read

You do not need perfect certainty to choose

Why can one small decision suddenly feel like a meeting with twelve very opinionated versions of ourselves? One wants to be practical. One wants to keep everyone happy. One has prepared a detailed presentation about everything that could possibly go wrong. And somewhere beneath all that noise, there may be a quieter voice saying, “I think I know what matters to me.”

We often wait for certainty to arrive before we choose. We imagine the right answer will feel bright and obvious, with no doubt attached to it. But many meaningful decisions do not come that way. They arrive with mixed feelings. Relief beside sadness. Excitement beside fear. A hopeful yes that still understands what it is leaving behind.

That does not mean you are choosing badly. It means the choice matters.

When the mind begins circling, it can help to step away from the question of which option is perfect. Perfect is a very demanding companion. Instead, ask what feels honest for the person you are today. What supports your values? What asks you to abandon yourself? What might be difficult, but still feel clean and true when you imagine living with it?

Your body may offer information too, though it does not have to deliver a dramatic sign. Notice what happens when you say each possibility quietly. Perhaps your shoulders soften. Perhaps you feel resistance because something is unfamiliar, not because it is wrong. Give yourself enough time to tell the difference. A pause can hold wisdom without becoming a hiding place.

And please remember: changing your mind is not automatically a failure of character. Sometimes new information appears. Sometimes a season changes, a relationship changes, or you change. A path that once fit may begin to pinch like shoes bought for a former version of your life. You are allowed to notice. You are allowed to choose again. 🌿

Of course, every choice has consequences, and kindness includes considering how our decisions affect others. But caring about someone is different from handing them the final vote on your life. You can listen with love, explain with respect, and still keep the responsibility for your own direction. That is not coldness. It is a mature kind of self-respect.

If there is a decision waiting for you today, try making the next honest move instead of solving the entire future. Write the email draft. Gather one piece of information. Take a walk before answering. Speak with someone who helps you hear yourself rather than someone who simply tells you what to do. Then let the answer breathe for a while. Not every quiet moment needs to be filled with another list of possibilities. Small steps can reveal what endless thinking keeps hidden.

You may not receive a guarantee. Most of us would love one; apparently the universe misplaced that department. ✨ But you can meet uncertainty with presence. You can choose thoughtfully, remain open, and trust yourself to respond to what comes next. A meaningful life is not built by always knowing. It is built by learning to stay close to yourself while you decide.

Carry this into today

What is the next honest step—not the perfect answer—you can take today? 🤍

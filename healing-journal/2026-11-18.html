A moment for yourself

November 18, 2026 · Reconnecting with yourself & everyday preferences · 3 min read

Let a small preference point the way

A surprisingly large part of the day can be spent answering questions that are not quite ours. What time works for everyone? What needs doing first? Which option makes the most sense? By evening, someone asks what you would like, and your mind produces the emotional equivalent of a loading circle. Apparently choosing a sandwich can become the final exam after a busy Tuesday. ✨

There is nothing wrong with being flexible. Sharing plans, considering other people, and adapting when circumstances change are generous parts of living together. But when flexibility becomes the only voice in the room, your own preferences can grow very quiet. Not gone. Just waiting somewhere behind the practical answer, hoping to be consulted before the meeting ends.

A preference does not have to be important to be real. You may like the window open. You may want the blue cup, the quieter route, or ten minutes without conversation when you arrive home. These choices do not need a persuasive argument. They are small pieces of information about how you are meeting this particular day. 🌿

Sometimes we distrust what we want because it changes. Yesterday you wanted company; today you would rather read alone. Last month a routine felt comforting, and now it feels crowded. This does not make you inconsistent in some alarming way. A living person responds to weather, energy, experience, and season. Even a garden asks for different care after rain than it does after heat.

Begin with something low-stakes. Before reaching for the familiar answer, pause and notice what sounds pleasant, useful, or kind to you now. Choose the music for one drive. Decide whether the lamp or the overhead light feels better. Order the flavor you actually want instead of the one that is easiest to share. No committee report is required. ⭐

Your preference will not always decide the outcome. Money, time, access, promises, and other people's needs are real parts of a choice. Sometimes you will willingly choose what serves the whole group. The difference is that you can include yourself in the decision. You can know what you wanted, name it when appropriate, and make a conscious compromise instead of disappearing automatically.

There may also be rooms where expressing a preference does not feel safe or where a simple choice is treated like a challenge. You are not required to practice honesty in the least welcoming place. Start privately if that is what fits: write down what you would choose, make one decision that belongs only to you, or share with someone who can hear a wish without turning it into a debate. 🌙

Over time, these modest choices can become a way of recognizing your own voice again. Not a loud declaration, not a promise to get your way, but a familiar inner sound: this appeals to me; that does not; I need a little more time. Let one preference have a place at the table today. It may seem as small as choosing where to sit, yet the gesture carries something tender—you remembered that your experience belongs in your life too. 🤍

Carry this into today

What small preference could you notice and honor today?

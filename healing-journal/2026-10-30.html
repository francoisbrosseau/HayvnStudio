A moment for yourself

October 30, 2026 · Everyday joy & permission · 3 min read

Use the good candle on an ordinary evening

There are certain things we become strangely good at saving. The candle that smells like a tiny enchanted forest. The beautiful cup pushed behind the practical mugs. The soft sweater kept for a day when nothing might spill—which, honestly, is a very ambitious standard for being human. We call these things special, then protect them so carefully that we rarely let them make life feel special at all. ✨

Saving something beautiful can be sensible. It can also become a quiet habit of postponing pleasure until the calendar produces an occasion worthy of it. Yet most of a life is made of ordinary mornings, ordinary errands, ordinary dinners, and evenings when everyone is a little tired. If joy may only enter wearing formal shoes, it will miss quite a few invitations.

Of course, this is not an instruction to use everything at once, spend beyond your means, or turn every Tuesday into a festival with a cleanup crew. Everyday joy does not need to be expensive or impressive. It may be fresh sheets, music while you cook, perfume before a solo walk, or the last bit of daylight enjoyed without also answering messages. 🌙

Small pleasures matter because they return you to the life that is actually here. Not the polished future version. Not the perfect weekend. Here. A cup warmed between your hands. A song that makes your shoulders move before your dignity can object. A meal placed on the plate you usually reserve for company, as though you are not also someone worth welcoming.

Try choosing one good thing today and allowing it to be used. Light the candle. Wear the earrings. Open the notebook with the lovely cover and risk putting an imperfect sentence on the first page. Beauty is not ruined by becoming part of your life; sometimes that is how it finally fulfills its purpose. A crystal ball is only glass until someone gives it a little light and imagination—symbolism, not a promise of magic. 🔮

There may be tenderness in this, too. Perhaps you learned to wait because resources were uncertain, because pleasure felt frivolous, or because caring for everyone else always came first. You do not need to judge that instinct. It may have protected something important. You can thank it, then gently decide that delight is allowed to have a small place in the present.

The stars do not save all their brightness for a grand event. They appear on unremarkable nights, above grocery lists, unfinished conversations, and laundry waiting in a chair. Let that be a simple reminder: an ordinary day does not have to earn every beautiful thing it receives. Some moments are precious because we notice them, not because they arrived with a ceremony. ⭐

Today, resist the urge to postpone one modest pleasure. Let it sit beside the practical parts of the day without apologizing for taking up room. Your responsibilities will still be there. The world will continue turning. But perhaps the evening will feel warmer, your meal will look kinder, or you will remember that joy is not only a reward given after everything is finished. It is also one of the ways we stay present while life is happening. 🌟

Carry this into today

What small, beautiful thing could you stop saving and enjoy as part of your real life today? 🤍

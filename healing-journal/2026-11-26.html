A moment for yourself

November 26, 2026 · Memory & the details we keep · 3 min read

Write down the part the recipe leaves out

Somewhere in a kitchen drawer there may be a recipe written in handwriting you could pick out of a crowd. The instructions are magnificent in their confidence. Add enough flour. Bake until done. Whoever wrote them clearly imagined standing beside you forever, pointing at the bowl when you got it right. Even after the quantities have been worked out, those few crooked lines can be the part you want to keep. 🌿

There are lovely things we know about people simply because we have spent time near them. How a friend pronounces the name of her hometown. Which corner of a blanket a child rubs between two fingers. The little upward whistle someone makes while looking for a parking space. It can be a pleasure to give one of those details a home somewhere outside our heads.

Try writing a sentence about somebody you love while the detail is fresh. A grocery receipt will do beautifully. Perhaps your sister cuts strawberries into impossibly thin slices so the last two berries can reach every plate. You might write just that. There is already so much of her in that small decision. You can leave the grand explanation of what family means for another afternoon. ✨

There is tenderness in asking someone how they do a familiar thing, too. If the person with the recipe is around, bring the bowl over. Ask what enough flour feels like. Watch for the moment they stop stirring and put their hand in. You might discover that the dough should feel like the soft part of an ear. Nobody was going to get that from a measuring cup.

Of course, some details belong to a time you cannot visit again. A particular laugh might come back whole one morning and feel impossibly far away the next. If writing it down hurts today, put the pencil away. There is no attendance sheet for remembering someone. You are allowed to eat the strawberries, rinse your plate and get on with the ordinary business of Thursday. 🤍

And perhaps the person whose ways deserve a line on a scrap of paper is sitting right where you are. Think of something your hands have learned to do. You know the angle that persuades the sticky gate to open. You can tell by the sound when the coffee is nearly ready. What would you tell a person spending their first morning in your kitchen? You have a whole vocabulary of small kindnesses that a visitor has yet to learn.

A scrap like that can travel surprisingly far. It can live inside a cookbook, find its way into a moving box, or turn up in a coat pocket when the weather changes. Imagine being handed one on a completely ordinary day. Someone noticed the way you do something. They liked it enough to remember. That is a sweet thing to know while both of you are here to smile about it. 🌙

Today, if a detail comes to mind, give it one honest sentence. Spell the nickname the funny way. Draw the bowl if words feel awkward. Leave enough space underneath for somebody else to add what they remember.
